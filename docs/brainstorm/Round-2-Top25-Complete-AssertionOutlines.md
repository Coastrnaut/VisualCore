# VisualCore — Refined Round 2: 25 Complete AssertionOutlines

**As of:** 2026-11-10 | **Scope:** selection from 500 scored casual 3D games | **Build rule:** 100% procedural VisualCore/GLSL/ANGLE/Metal graphics | **Price:** $1.99–$4.99 paid once, **never ads**.

## Reading guide: argument, reasons, evidence, counterarguments

Each game has **exactly three arguable claims**, each with **four reasons**, and each reason with **one explicitly identified evidence item**: 3 × 4 = **12 reason–evidence pairs per game**. Totals: **75 claims, 300 reasons, 300 evidence items**. Arguments are hypotheses to test, not fabricated proof of revenues, excellent graphics, or completed performance benchmarks.

**Evidence-strength legend:** **Observed external** means a reputable public source confirms something about a comparison category or technology; **Analytical/design** means the current written design exhibits the property but a playable build does not yet; **Acceptance contract** is a falsifiable planned test, not a passed test. Market analogs demonstrate possibilities, never sales of the proposed games.

**Do not confuse an argument with a promise:** a company can reject a compelling argument if prototypes, real-device metrics, or acquisition tests falsify its premises. A game must not be called a success because it receives a high heuristic score.

## 01. Aurora Loom — 94.80/100

**Candidate:** `G162` | **Family:** Light and color | **Premium price:** $2.99 | **Visual assessment:** 97.83/100 | **Comparative paid-game score:** 87.73/100.

**Playing fantasy:** weave and phase-shift 3D luminous ribbons into target harmonies. **The moment of wonder:** layered ribbon meshes with wave displacement and fragment interference patterns.

### Claim 1 — The signature visual system is a plausible code-only VisualCore product

**Claim:** A compelling first impression for **Aurora Loom** can plausibly be rendered entirely by GLSL ES and generated geometry, because its primary spectacle is produced by the same rules that players manipulate. This is a feasibility assertion, not a benchmark result.

1. **Reason:** The visible world is expressible as deterministic procedural meshes and shaders, so the design avoids painted hero backgrounds and manual polygon modeling.
   **Evidence:** **Analytical/design:** the candidate rendering specification proposes “Layered ribbon meshes with wave displacement and fragment interference patterns”; that is an executable algorithmic direction, not a completed screenshot.

2. **Reason:** The project reuses the existing OpenGL ES → ANGLE → Metal path instead of inventing another GPU backend for strong moving geometry with responsive lighting.
   **Evidence:** **Observed external technical:** Google documents ANGLE as an OpenGL ES implementation with an iOS Metal backend ([ANGLE repository](https://github.com/google/angle)); this establishes interface viability, not device frame rate.

3. **Reason:** Every hero object can be made from meshes, particles, shader gradients or procedural fields; Aurora Loom does not require an image model or character animator.
   **Evidence:** **Analytical/design:** the declared content bill of materials contains zero generated-image assets, zero painted textures and zero manually rigged characters; a later repository scan must verify that promise.

4. **Reason:** The renderer can be inspected with repeatable, fixed-seed camera captures and performance counters before art quality is accepted.
   **Evidence:** **Observed external technical:** Apple describes GPU-time, frame pacing and thermal inspection in its [game-performance guidance](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/meet-with-apple/242/); a proposed gate uses the same categories on D3.


### Claim 2 — The mechanic deserves a serious test as a paid-once, no-ad casual game

**Claim:** There is a commercially defensible hypothesis for selling **Aurora Loom** at $2.99 without ads, because the loop has a clear objective, a polished visual payoff and relevant premium-game precedents. This is not a revenue forecast.

1. **Reason:** The loop is understandable as a concrete player action: “Weave and phase-shift 3D luminous ribbons into target harmonies.”
   **Evidence:** **Analytical/design:** a prospective tutorial can show the action and a visible success state in fewer than three gestures; the loop is written in the candidate inventory, but has not yet been tested with players.

2. **Reason:** Players demonstrably purchase some complete iPhone/iPad games rather than only ad-funded products.
   **Evidence:** **Observed external market:** Apple’s [official 2025 U.S. paid iPhone chart](https://apps.apple.com/us/iphone/room/6756363168) lists titles including Balatro, Geometry Dash and Stardew Valley; none proves demand for Aurora Loom.

3. **Reason:** The genre has a neighboring PC example, suggesting a category audience worth examining without copying its protected presentation.
   **Evidence:** **Observed external market:** the [published comparable: Glass Masquerade official Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/511470/Glass_Masquerade/) provides a real title in a related category; reviews and price are not evidence of sales for Aurora Loom.

4. **Reason:** A one-price offline product removes the need for rewarded videos, accounts, subscription churn and paid virtual goods.
   **Evidence:** **Analytical/design:** the business model is exactly $2.99 upfront with all stages unlocked, no network services, no analytics advertising identifiers, and no IAP dependencies; validate purchaser acceptance before release.


### Claim 3 — A bounded prototype can decide whether this game merits production

**Claim:** **Aurora Loom** is worth a controlled engineering prototype rather than an immediate 25-app launch commitment, because its risks can be made explicit and measured against falsifiable criteria.

1. **Reason:** The game can distinguish itself from directly similar procedural puzzle games with its actual interaction, rather than relying on a palette swap.
   **Evidence:** **Analytical/design:** the proposed distinguishing action is “Weave and phase-shift 3D luminous ribbons into target harmonies,” with the spectacle “Layered ribbon meshes with wave displacement and fragment interference patterns”; the final originality and trademark review remains unperformed.

2. **Reason:** Seeded rules can produce a complete offline collection of repeatable seeded goals and standalone sessions without a large handmade level-art pipeline.
   **Evidence:** **Analytical/design:** the content prototype is ten seeded frequency settings whose targets can be evaluated by a numeric tolerance; its generator and fun factor must still be implemented and verified.

3. **Reason:** A reliable automated test can separately determine logical solvability and scene beauty rather than conflating a screenshot with gameplay.
   **Evidence:** **Acceptance contract (not observed):** pass only after level replay agrees with the solver at fixed seeds, screenshot comparisons reveal no missing geometry, and a human reviewer can identify the goal in a blind five-second view.

4. **Reason:** The expensive uncertainty is observable on real hardware: thermal and overdraw risk on D3; crowding among optical/geometry premium games.
   **Evidence:** **Acceptance contract (not observed):** after full-suite implementation lock, run a 30-minute session on D3 iPad A16/iPadOS 26.5, and record GPU frame timing, missed frames, thermals, visual captures and app crashes; reject or downscale if failed.


**Strongest objection:** This may be a beautiful technical demonstration with too little repeatable fun, or a near-duplicate of another VisualCore game. The high score cannot answer that objection.

**Response:** Keep development gated: build the rule “Weave and phase-shift 3D luminous ribbons into target harmonies” before polishing a trailer; measure solving, retries and user comprehension; compare the candidate against other shortlisted mechanisms.

**Kill / pivot criteria:** Stop or combine this idea if a seeded level has no meaningful choice, if real D3 renders fail sustained budgets after quality scaling, if distinctive visual assets require manual art, or if independent players cannot explain the rules. Do not publish near-identical apps to inflate the portfolio.

**Argument verdict:** **PROTOTYPE, NOT GUARANTEED LAUNCH.** The central claim is that a beautiful and interactive code-generated game is *testable*; its commercial desirability is still unproven.
